Background & Purpose

Japan permits a supplier to use an alternative chemical name in certain statutory notifications when disclosure of an ingredient's chemical identity would reveal a trade secret, provided the alternative does not obstruct chemical risk assessment and the resulting protective measures. This balances commercial confidentiality against the information workers and employers need to handle hazardous substances safely.

The Industrial Safety and Health Act also protects medical access to the true ingredient name when needed for diagnosis. The current record-preservation system requires businesses using alternative names to prepare and preserve records; when a business closes during the preservation period, the records are transferred to the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office chief. MHLW says this arrangement needs adjustment so that doctors' requests can be handled promptly even after the business has ceased operations.

Sources: [1] [2]

Legislative & Regulatory History

Under Article 57-2 of the Industrial Safety and Health Act, use of an alternative name is accompanied by record-preservation duties and a duty to respond to a doctor's disclosure request in the circumstances specified by law. MHLW's outline identifies Ministerial Ordinance No. 3 of 2026 as the basis for the existing record creation, preservation and business-closure handover framework, effective from 1 April 2026.

On 27 October 2026, MHLW opened public consultation on a new draft. It proposes changing the recipient of records on business closure from the competent Labour Standards Inspection Office chief to a minister-designated alternative-chemical-name records preservation body. The designation criteria, the body's operational duties, business rules and ministerial oversight would be specified in the registration/designation ordinance.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Suppliers that rely on alternative chemical names would need to identify the legally designated custodian before closing operations if the proposal is adopted. The intended change concerns where protected records go, not a blanket requirement to disclose trade-secret chemical identities publicly.

For doctors seeking information for diagnosis, the proposed designated body is intended to provide continuity after the original supplier closes. Whether the operational mechanism achieves timely access depends on the final ordinance and the designated body's procedures.

Points to check

  • Identify whether the business uses alternative chemical names under Article 57-2 and already maintains the associated records.
  • For potential business closures, compare the current handover recipient with the proposed designated custodian and check the consultation outline before submitting comments.
