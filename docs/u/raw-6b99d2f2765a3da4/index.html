Background & Purpose

Japan's regional lenders face shrinking local populations, changing demand for branches and payment services, and high fixed costs for maintaining IT infrastructure. The Financial Services Agency identified the need to preserve financial services while enabling institutions to strengthen their operating foundations. The policy problem is not simply a shortfall of capital: institutions must also fund restructuring and systems that can serve smaller markets efficiently.

Earlier public support focused on capital participation and assistance linked to mergers or restructuring. The 2026 reform broadens the toolkit to cover the design and development of jointly used information systems and revises the time limits and scope of existing arrangements. This connects the policy objective of maintaining regional access to finance with concrete consolidation and digital-infrastructure costs.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The Cabinet submitted the bill on 27 February 2026. During the Diet's April deliberations, the government identified population decline and the strengthening of regional lenders' management foundations as the reason for the measure. The Diet passed the bill on 24 April 2026, and Act No. 15 was promulgated on 7 May 2026.

The Financial Services Agency consulted on implementing rules in May and June 2026 and published the results on 24 June. Related orders, ordinances and supervisory guidance took effect or began to apply on 25 June 2026. The former standalone financial-function strengthening guideline was withdrawn and relevant material incorporated into the comprehensive supervisory guidelines.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Institutions considering integration or a shared core system can now assess the specific grant route for qualifying joint-system development, rather than assuming that only a merger can be supported. The regulations define eligible institution categories and project types; their applicability is fact-dependent.

Financial and technology counterparties should distinguish a policy incentive for shared infrastructure from a general mandate to merge or outsource systems. The amendment creates support mechanisms; it does not itself compel a regional bank to join a common platform.

Points to check

  • Identify whether the institution and planned joint-system project fall within the categories specified in the implementing rules.
  • Compare merger-related assistance with joint-system assistance, including eligible costs and application requirements.
  • Use the current comprehensive supervisory guidelines rather than the withdrawn standalone financial-function strengthening guideline.
