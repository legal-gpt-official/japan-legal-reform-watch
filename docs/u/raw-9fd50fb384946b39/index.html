Cabinet Order No. 327 of 2026 was promulgated on 15 October 2026 to implement parts of the 2025 Environmental Impact Assessment Act amendment. Background & Purpose

The 2025 amendment to Japan's Environmental Impact Assessment Act was designed, among other things, to clarify environmental assessment for repowering: removal or decommissioning of an existing facility followed by construction of a similar facility on the original or a nearby site. This responds to the practical difficulty of applying a framework chiefly designed around greenfield development to replacement projects, while preserving scrutiny of environmental impacts. The Ministry of the Environment's October 2026 briefing expressly links the implementing order to this 2025 legislative choice.

Under amended Article 3-3(2), the geographic and relative-size limits for the special repowering planning-stage consideration document are set by Cabinet Order. Order No. 327 of 2026 fixes 300 metres from the existing site's boundary for thermal, geothermal and wind power plant construction or modification, alongside a new-to-existing generating-capacity ratio no greater than 2.0 for thermal, 1.3 for geothermal and 1.1 for wind. These are conditions for the specified repowering category, not universal limits on all generation upgrades or substitutes for other EIA triggers.

The order also defines first- and second-category project criteria for dam reconstruction. Developers must distinguish this October 2026 repowering implementation order from the different July 2026 Order No. 230 concerning the same base Enforcement Order: sharing a parent statute does not make the separate legal amendments one dossier.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The 217th Diet enacted the Environmental Impact Assessment Act amendment as Act No. 73 of 2025 in June 2025. In October 2025 the Ministry began technical discussions on applying the new repowering provisions, including geographic proximity and relative capacity; those discussions informed the draft implementing rules.

From 25 August to 23 September 2026, the Ministry consulted the public on the proposed Enforcement Order and an associated basic-guidance notice (consultation 195260051). The Ministry publicly released the consultation results, including 71 submitted comments, on 9 October 2026, when it also announced Cabinet approval of the Order and the separate commencement-date order.

The EIA Enforcement Order amendment was promulgated as Cabinet Order No. 327 of 2026 on 15 October 2026; e-Gov's revision record states that it enters into force on 1 April 2027. The Ministry's 9 October press release alone documents Cabinet approval rather than promulgation; the latter is confirmed separately by the e-Gov law-revision record. Promulgation and future commencement must not be conflated.

Expected procedural steps

The usual procedure for this kind of instrument; not a forecast for this reform.

  • Project proponents should assess whether future replacement work falls within the statutory repowering category and its 300-metre and 2.0/1.3/1.1 output conditions, and should prepare the applicable environmental review and consultations under the provisions that take effect on 1 April 2027.
  • The Ministry may issue additional technical guidance and implementation materials. Any such publications, and the enacted text rather than the August draft, should be used when determining actual procedural treatment.

Outstanding uncertainties

  • Whether an individual project qualifies depends on the statutory description, site boundaries, same-kind facility test, generation output calculation, other EIA thresholds and any applicable transitional rules; the distance/ratio figures alone do not conclusively decide a project's required EIA procedure.
  • The effects on a specific dam reconstruction project require checking its first- or second-category classification under the amended tables; no blanket exemption or permit approval follows merely from this order.
  • Future ministerial guidance may elaborate evidence required for project screening; it cannot be assumed from the cited sources.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Wind, geothermal and thermal developers planning to replace existing equipment should map original and replacement site boundaries, calculate comparable nameplate generating capacity and document why a project qualifies, or does not qualify, for the repowering provisions. The 1.1 wind ratio is materially narrower than the 2.0 thermal ratio; using a single threshold across technologies would distort project screening.

EIA advisors and project-finance teams should reflect the 1 April 2027 commencement in permit schedules, land documentation and due-diligence assumptions. For dam reconstruction, confirm first/second-category classification separately. The new order does not dispense with other licensing, nature-conservation, land or grid requirements.

Points to check

  • Map the existing facility boundary and proposed project layout; assess the 300-metre limit under the amended statutory definition.
  • Compute the new-to-existing output ratio using the applicable thermal 2.0, geothermal 1.3 or wind 1.1 threshold; record source data and methodology.
  • Check project classification, any planning-stage consideration document requirements, other EIA triggers and transitional provisions against the promulgated texts effective 1 April 2027.
  • Confirm dam-reconstruction first/second-category thresholds if relevant; monitor Ministry technical guidance without mistaking guidance for a new legal duty.
