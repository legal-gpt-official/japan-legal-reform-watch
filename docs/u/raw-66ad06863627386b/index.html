Background & Purpose

The bill's sponsors argue that higher prices and insufficient wage growth leave workers exposed to both taxes and social insurance contributions. Raising income-tax deductions may help taxpayers who owe tax, but it cannot fully relieve workers with little taxable income who nevertheless pay social contributions.

The proposed design therefore combines two tools: deductions for income tax and individual inhabitant tax, and payments to certain workers to supplement tax relief. It also aims to reduce work-hour disincentives associated with contribution burdens. The proposal's rationale is distinct from a finding that every current deduction or insurance contribution is unlawful.

The bill would seek to protect local-government finances against revenue losses caused by changing inhabitant-tax deductions. This reveals a fiscal trade-off: relieving households without transferring the cost of national tax policy to municipalities. Precise payment eligibility and amounts would depend on subsequent legal measures.

Legislative & Regulatory History

Debate over the income threshold for taxation and the interaction of taxes, social insurance and working hours formed the background to the proposal. The bill's text frames relief as a response to living-cost pressure rather than as a standalone reduction of social insurance contribution rates.

Four members submitted House Bill No. 1 in the 222nd Diet on 20 October 2026. It proposes a combined maximum basic and minimum employment-income deduction of at least 1,780,000 yen for income tax, with related individual inhabitant-tax reforms and a payment mechanism for workers.

The House's legislative progress record checked on 31 October shows submission but no enactment or promulgation. The proposed 2027 and 2028 tax-year measures and payments remain legislative objectives, not instructions to employers to change withholding today.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Payroll operators should distinguish current income-tax withholding, social insurance contribution calculations and inhabitant-tax collection from the proposed future reforms. A proposal for a 1,780,000 yen combined deduction is not itself a current tax-free salary threshold for every worker.

If enacted, employers might need to adjust payroll communications and systems, while municipalities would have to administer changes subject to compensation arrangements. The business impact depends on subsequent legislation and detailed payment procedures.

Points to check

  • Do not change current payroll withholding or social insurance deductions on the basis of the unpassed bill.
  • Model income-tax deductions, inhabitant-tax deductions and cash transfers as separate mechanisms rather than a single uniform threshold.
  • Monitor the House record and any enacted implementing tax legislation before updating employee-facing guidance.
