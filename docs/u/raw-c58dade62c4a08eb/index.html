Background & Purpose

Japan's 2025 transaction-law reform responded to increases in labor, materials and energy costs and sought structural pass-through of higher prices across supply chains. The existing promotion framework encourages cooperation between commissioning businesses and smaller suppliers, but a legal prohibition on unfair price-setting does not by itself supply skills, information or administrative capacity to a small supplier.

The 2026 members' bill takes a complementary approach: it would place the commissioning firm's understanding of fair-remuneration conditions among examples of cooperative promotion projects, add workforce development to promotion standards, and call for coordinated assistance when smaller suppliers apply for public subsidies. These are proposed support and policy measures, not an enacted new private-law right to a particular price.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The government had already advanced a 2025 amendment addressing price discussions, payment instruments and covered transactions. That earlier reform provides the context for the later proposal, but the two must not be conflated: the 2026 bill would amend the promotion statute's support architecture rather than automatically re-enact the transaction prohibitions.

House Bill No. 32 of the 221st Diet was submitted on 15 July 2026. The House's current bill record shows referral to the Economy, Trade and Industry Committee on 5 October, with no recorded passage or promulgation. Its published outline specifies the proposed additions to promotion plans, promotion standards and cooperation among support bodies.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

If enacted, a commissioning business developing a promotion plan could face stronger policy expectations to understand how suppliers' remuneration is determined, while smaller suppliers and support organizations could gain more structured assistance for training and grant applications. The draft does not establish a universal duty for every buyer to audit each supplier's price.

Companies should keep separate their obligations under existing transaction law and any prospective cooperative-support measures. The main policy question is whether fair-pricing commitments can be supported by supplier capabilities and access to assistance, rather than merely documented in contracts.

Points to check

  • Map which supplier relationships fall under existing transaction rules and which are candidates for voluntary promotion plans.
  • Identify whether supplier remuneration reviews and training support are feasible within existing supplier-development programs.
  • Track the House committee record before describing proposed assistance as a statutory entitlement.
