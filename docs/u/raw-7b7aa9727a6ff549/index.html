At a glance

What Changed?

METI Ordinance No. 64 of 2026, promulgated and effective on 31 July 2026, added special underwriting to the definitions in Article 1 of the Enforcement Regulation of the Trade Insurance Act and inserted Article 5-2, which prescribes what NEXI must file when it seeks ministerial approval of a special underwriting decision. [2] [3] [4] [6]

Who Is Affected?

The filing duty binds NEXI alone. Exporters, lenders and project sponsors are affected indirectly: official cover for a transaction under a government-to-government arrangement designated by the METI Minister is available only through this approval-gated special underwriting route, not under the ordinary underwriting lines. [2] [3]

What Should Businesses Do Now?

Parties structuring a transaction for cover under a designated arrangement should confirm the designation and the published special underwriting standards first, and build the METI Minister's prior approval, which NEXI must obtain for each decision, into the transaction timetable as a separate government decision point. Background & Purpose

Act No. 29 of 2026 created a new category in the Trade Insurance Act (Act No. 67 of 1950): special underwriting, under which Nippon Export and Investment Insurance (NEXI) underwrites trade insurance or reinsurance connected with an arrangement between the Japanese government and a foreign government that the Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry designates as particularly necessary to strengthen the resilience of Japanese companies' supply chains, whether by creating overseas demand for Japanese machinery and other products or by securing stable supplies of important materials. METI's official explanation of the bill presents the scheme as a response to supply-chain vulnerability exposed by geopolitical risk, and as designed with the Japan-United States strategic investment initiative in view, following a framework agreement between the two governments in July 2025 and a memorandum of understanding on strategic investment signed on 4 September 2025.

The Diet surrounded the new business with safeguards that ordinary underwriting does not have. The Minister must set and publish special underwriting business guidelines covering implementation, financial management and reporting (Article 16-2 of the Act) and special underwriting standards framed by those guidelines (Article 16-3). NEXI must decide each special underwriting in accordance with the standards and must obtain the Minister's approval before deciding (Article 16-4). The business is accounted for separately from NEXI's other business (Article 20-2) in a dedicated special account (Article 38-2), and the Act's supplementary provisions allow the government, until 31 March 2029, to issue and deliver to NEXI interest-free government bonds of up to 3 trillion yen to keep that account sound and to fund claims payments. The design keeps strategically motivated, potentially large and concentrated exposures visible and separately financed instead of letting them rest on the ordinary trade insurance account.

Ordinance No. 64 of 2026 is the procedural piece of that design at regulation level. Article 1 of the Enforcement Regulation now defines special underwriting by reference to Article 16-2(1) of the Act. New Article 5-2 provides that when NEXI seeks the Minister's approval of a special underwriting decision under Article 16-4(2) of the Act, it must submit an application to the Minister stating two things: the content of the trade insurance or reinsurance concerned, and the matters needed to confirm whether the underwriting relates to an arrangement designated by the Minister under Article 16-2(1). The ordinance was one of five instruments METI prepared for the new business, alongside Ordinance No. 65 of 2026 on NEXI's accounting and Notices Nos. 96, 97 and 98 of 2026 designating the qualifying arrangement and publishing the guidelines and the standards; the consultation documents cite Articles 30, 16-2(1) and 16-3(1) of the Act as the package's legal bases.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The reform began as part of a wider METI bill to amend the Industrial Competitiveness Enhancement Act and related statutes. The Cabinet approved the bill and the government submitted it to the 221st Diet on 6 March 2026. The Diet enacted it on 29 May 2026, and it was promulgated as Act No. 29 of 2026 on 5 June 2026; its amendments to the Trade Insurance Act were set to commence on 31 July 2026.

METI then prepared the subordinate instruments. A public consultation on the five draft instruments, including Ordinance No. 64 of 2026, ran from 22 June 2026 to 21 July 2026. METI published the results on 31 July 2026: two comments had been submitted, no change was made in response to comments, and only non-substantive technical revisions were made to the drafts. All five instruments were promulgated on 31 July 2026, and the Trade Insurance Act amendments and Ordinance No. 64 of 2026 took effect the same day. Ordinance No. 64 of 2026 carries no transitional provisions of its own; the amendment Act's supplementary provisions separately direct that underwriting done before commencement which corresponds to the special underwriting business be accounted for in the special account.

Expected procedural steps

The usual procedure for this kind of instrument; not a forecast for this reform.

  • Individual transactions under a designated arrangement are expected to be examined case by case against the special underwriting standards, with a ministerial approval decision for each underwriting.
  • Further arrangements could be designated by later ministerial notices if the government concludes new arrangements that meet the statutory purpose.

Outstanding uncertainties

  • The operative texts of Notices Nos. 96, 97 and 98 of 2026 were not re-inspected article by article for this explainer. The consultation proposed designating the Japan-United States memorandum of understanding on strategic investment, and METI reported only non-substantive technical revisions on promulgation; the exact scope of the designation should be confirmed in the notice text before it is relied on.
  • No individual special underwriting approval has been confirmed in the official materials checked for this explainer, so approval practice, processing time and any conditions remain untested.
  • Ordinance No. 65 of 2026 amended the separate METI ordinance on NEXI's accounting; its detailed accounting rules are outside the scope of this explainer.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

For exporters, lenders and project sponsors, the practical change is a new route to Japanese official cover for transactions that sit under a designated government-to-government arrangement. Eligibility turns on the arrangement's designation and on the published special underwriting standards rather than on the ordinary underwriting criteria, so a transaction that would not fit the ordinary lines may still be coverable, and a transaction outside any designated arrangement gains nothing from the new scheme. Because each decision requires the METI Minister's prior approval on NEXI's Article 5-2 filing, deal timetables should treat that approval as a distinct government decision point whose timing is not within the parties' control.

For NEXI and for users of the ordinary trade insurance lines, the ring-fencing matters: special underwriting is kept in a separate special account, reported on separately, and supported by interest-free government bonds of up to 3 trillion yen that the government may deliver until 31 March 2029. Strategic exposures taken on for supply-chain policy reasons are therefore financed and disclosed on their own footing instead of being absorbed silently into the ordinary account.

Points to check

  • Confirm whether the transaction falls under an arrangement designated by the METI Minister under Article 16-2(1) of the Trade Insurance Act, using the designation notice text.
  • Read the published special underwriting business guidelines and special underwriting standards before assuming a transaction is coverable.
  • Build NEXI's application and the METI Minister's prior approval under Article 16-4(2) into the transaction timetable as a separate step.
  • Do not assume ordinary trade insurance terms, criteria or timelines carry over to special underwriting; the business is separately accounted for in the special account.
  • Monitor METI and NEXI announcements for the first approvals and for any further designated arrangements.
