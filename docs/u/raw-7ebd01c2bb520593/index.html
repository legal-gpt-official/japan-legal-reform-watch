Background & Purpose

Japan's economic security policy increasingly treats overseas supply-chain and strategic investment as matters of national resilience. JBIC explained that some strategically important overseas projects face uncertain profitability, so existing finance tools alone may not induce sufficient private investment.

The new approach links government recognition of qualifying overseas projects with JBIC support, including subordinated equity and a separate account. This is a risk-sharing policy instrument, not a general guarantee that all overseas projects will receive public financing.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The Diet enacted amendments to the Economic Security Promotion Act and JBIC Act on 10 June 2026. The new legal architecture responded to rapidly changing international conditions and the perceived investment gap for strategic overseas projects.

Following consultations on the subordinate instruments in August 2026, Cabinet Order No. 290 and the joint Cabinet Office-Finance Ministry Ordinance No. 5 were promulgated on 16 September and took effect on 18 September. JBIC said that actual operations under the new account still require budget measures and preparation.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Companies developing certified strategic overseas projects may eventually obtain a form of public risk capital that complements private lenders. Eligibility, project certification and financing terms are separate decisions; the legal framework becoming effective does not itself approve any particular investment.

Sponsors and financiers should distinguish the legal commencement of the implementing instruments from JBIC's operational readiness and appropriations. This distinction matters for financing timetables, conditions precedent and assumptions in project agreements.

Points to check

  • Assess whether a proposed overseas project meets the government's strategic certification framework before modelling JBIC participation.
  • Confirm JBIC's program availability, budget arrangements and indicative terms before relying on subordinated investment in closing conditions.
