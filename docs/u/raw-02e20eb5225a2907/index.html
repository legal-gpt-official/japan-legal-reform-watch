Background & Purpose

Agrivoltaics promises both electricity generation and continuing crop production, but placing structures above agricultural land creates a persistent tension between renewable-energy development and protection of productive farmland. Japan's rural renewable-energy law already uses municipal basic plans and project recognition to coordinate energy development with agricultural interests.

The 2026 changes move the assessment of solar sharing more explicitly into that local planning and recognition structure. The Ministry's revised guidance asks municipalities to articulate how agrivoltaic projects preserve farming, and it addresses the relationship between the proposed installation, cultivation and surrounding agricultural land. The policy choice is to evaluate farming continuity and local land use rather than treating electricity production alone as sufficient.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The Ministry opened consultation on the draft ordinance in July 2026. MAFF Ordinance No. 72 was promulgated on 9 October 2026. e-Gov records show amendments to the rural renewable-energy enforcement regulation effective that day, while amendments affecting the Farmland Act enforcement regulation have a later effective date of 1 April 2027.

On 27 October 2026, the Ministry updated its operational guidance for the municipal planning system. That guidance discusses how municipal basic plans should address agrivoltaic installations, crop-production performance and evidence for exceptional configurations. The ordinance's different commencement dates must be tracked by amended regulation, not treated as a single blanket date.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Project developers may need to plan earlier for municipal engagement, cultivation evidence and a recognized project plan before relying on the farmland permission route after the scheduled commencement. Timing can affect land acquisition, financing conditions and construction sequencing.

Municipalities must balance solar deployment with agricultural production and efficient land use. The revised guidance permits evidence-based exceptions in some configurations; it does not support treating every agrivoltaic proposal as automatically acceptable.

Points to check

  • Identify whether the relevant application falls before or after the 1 April 2027 farmland-rule commencement.
  • Check whether the municipality's basic plan covers the proposed agrivoltaic site and farming arrangement.
  • Prepare the cultivation plan and supporting evidence in line with the Ministry's October 2026 operational guidance.
