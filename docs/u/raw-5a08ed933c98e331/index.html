Background & Purpose

The Cabinet Legislation Bureau explains that international conditions have increased the importance of secure energy and metal mineral supplies. The government proposes to strengthen the support available for Japanese companies to secure those resources.

The submitted text would expand JOGMEC's equity-investment authority to specified oil and natural-gas transport operations, including overseas pipelines and certain rail, road and maritime transport. It would also cover specified carbon dioxide capture, transport, storage and related geological exploration projects through investment and other support powers. These are proposed public support functions, not immediately binding obligations on private sponsors.

The proposal also sits alongside Japan's existing carbon storage policy, but it is a separate legislative initiative. METI reported in June 2024 that JOGMEC had selected nine advanced CCS projects aimed at developing replicable business models and starting operations by 2030. That earlier project selection illustrates the policy context; it does not establish eligibility for the new grants contemplated by the October 2026 bill.

The proposed new CCS grant authority is narrower than an unconditional subsidy: Article 11(1)(7-2) would cover funds necessary to continue specified capture, transport and storage projects where their particular business environment makes continuation difficult. Proposed Articles 18-2 and 18-3 would require METI to establish and publish grant criteria and JOGMEC to determine recipients and amounts under those criteria, subject to ministerial approval. The precise grant criteria have not yet been established.

What Happens Next

Confirmed next steps

No further step has been confirmed by an official source.

Expected procedural steps

The usual procedure for this kind of instrument; not a forecast for this reform.

  • The Diet may deliberate on and amend the bill. If enacted and promulgated, the proposed supplementary Article 1 would bring the main provisions into force on a date designated by Cabinet Order within three months after promulgation; the delegated transitional-measures provision would take effect on promulgation. Neither the three-month outer limit nor the bill's submission date is a fixed commencement date.

Outstanding uncertainties

  • The bill has no enacted amendment Act number or confirmed commencement date.
  • The proposed grant criteria, individual recipient and amount decisions, ministerial approvals, and implementation budgets are not established by the bill text. No entitlement to a grant follows merely from submitting a project.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Sponsors of qualifying oil and gas transport and CCS projects may evaluate possible future JOGMEC investment or grants. In particular, the proposed CCS grant targets continuity risks in specified projects and would depend on published ministerial criteria and approvals; the submitted bill creates no present right to funding.

Points to check

  • Check the official Diet status and amendments to the bill.
  • Assess whether planned oil and gas transport, or CCS capture, transport and storage, matches the geographically and operationally defined proposed functions.
  • Track enactment, any commencement Cabinet Order, and the proposed METI grant criteria and ministerial approvals before assuming support is available.
