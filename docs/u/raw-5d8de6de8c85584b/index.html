Background & Purpose

Medical equipment supply is a security issue when disruption can directly affect life-saving treatment. Japan's economic-security legislation allows specified critical products to be supported through recognised supply-security plans. The government designated ventilators as critical products in December 2025, extending supply resilience policy to an essential medical technology.

Recognition of a supply-security plan is not simply a subsidy application. The joint ordinance requires supporting corporate and financial documents, evidence of planned investments and measures for supply shortages, and procedures for amendments and reporting. This seeks to connect public support to a credible capacity to maintain supply in emergencies.

The 2026 amendment and the new application process are best understood as operationalising the designated-product framework. The policy balances the public interest in emergency access to ventilators against the cost of investing in domestic production and maintaining documented contingency arrangements.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The Economic Security Promotion Act established the supply-security planning framework. Following the government's December 2025 designation of ventilators, MHLW and METI published a joint ordinance and arrangements for recognition and support.

The ministries consulted on an amendment from 24 August to 4 September 2026, using a shortened consultation period explained by the need to prepare the scheme quickly for a critical product. The official consultation identified the recognition framework and the new statutory mechanism for requesting cooperation where supply is at risk.

e-Gov's amendment history records the 2026 joint ordinance amendment as promulgated and effective on 18 September 2026. The Ministry's current ventilator page explains that support requires a recognised plan and that application arrangements depend on the designation of a support organisation.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Ventilator manufacturers considering a supply-security plan may need to document production investment, alternate sourcing and supply-shortage response, together with corporate and financial records. Recognition can make specified assistance available, but the ordinance does not guarantee funding for every applicant.

Approved operators should distinguish material changes requiring fresh recognition from minor changes requiring notification, and monitor reporting duties. Purchasers of ventilators are not automatically required to submit supply-security plans merely because the product is designated.

Points to check

  • Confirm that the proposed ventilator production or supply activity falls within the current MHLW programme and recognition policy.
  • Prepare plan evidence for capacity investment, shortage response, finances and required permits before seeking recognition.
  • For recognised plans, distinguish changes requiring approval from minor notifications and scheduled implementation reports.
