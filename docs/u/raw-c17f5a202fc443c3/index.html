At a glance

What Changed?

Act No. 17 of 2026 raised statutory ceilings for agricultural modernization loans and widened the eligible-entity framework. The amended law took effect on 1 October 2026. [1] [2] [4]

Who Is Affected?

Farmers, agricultural corporations, agricultural cooperatives, qualifying entities linked to Norinchukin Bank, and financial institutions providing agricultural modernization loans. [1] [2] [4]

What Should Businesses Do Now?

Check the current financing rules and lender screening criteria. MAFF lists a JPY 200,000,000 ceiling for individual farmers and JPY 700,000,000 for agricultural corporations; these are ceilings, not guaranteed approvals. [1] [2] [4]

Background & Purpose

The amendment responds to growing demand for long-term, low-interest financing for farm expansion, productivity and value-added investment. It changes the Agricultural Modernization Financing Act rather than creating an unconditional right to borrow.

The Act raises the statutory aggregate-loan ceiling from JPY 200,000,000 to JPY 700,000,000 for the category of farmers specified by Cabinet Order. For other farmers, it raises the maximum amount that a Cabinet Order may set from JPY 40,000,000 to JPY 200,000,000. It also adds certain entities with Norinchukin Bank as a principal investor or participant, as specified by Cabinet Order, to the eligible-entity framework.

MAFF's current program information lists a JPY 200,000,000 borrowing ceiling for individual farmers and JPY 700,000,000 for agricultural corporations and groups, with a repayment period of up to 20 years. These published program terms must be distinguished from the statutory ceiling and from each lender's credit decision.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The Cabinet submitted the bill to the 221st Diet on 17 March 2026. The House of Representatives passed it on 16 April, and the House of Councillors passed it on 24 April. The amendment was promulgated on 7 May 2026 as Act No. 17 of 2026.

On 28 September 2026, e-Gov published the results of consultation on the implementing Cabinet Order, stating that the Order was promulgated that day and that the proposal was not revised following comments. e-Gov's law revision history confirms that the Act's amendment took effect on 1 October 2026.

What Happens Next

Confirmed next steps

No further step has been confirmed by an official source.

Outstanding uncertainties

  • Actual financing remains subject to eligibility, use-of-proceeds requirements, lender assessment and applicable interest-subsidy arrangements. A statutory or published program ceiling is not a promise of a loan.
  • Interest rates and operational program details can change. Borrowers and lenders should check MAFF's current program page and the applicable lender's latest terms before committing to a transaction.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Farm operators planning machinery, facilities or other qualifying investments can reassess funding options under the revised ceilings, but should verify the precise applicant category and eligible expenditure before assuming access to the higher amount.

Agricultural lenders and advisers should update eligibility screening, product disclosures and internal checklists to reflect the law in force and the implementing program rules. They should not present the statutory maximum as a universally available loan amount.

Points to check

  • Confirm whether the applicant is an individual farmer, agricultural corporation, cooperative or another eligible entity under the applicable rules.
  • Compare the planned borrowing with the current program ceiling and the lender's credit and security requirements.
  • Check permitted use of funds, repayment period, current interest rate and any interest-subsidy conditions.
  • Keep a record of the legal version, MAFF program information and lender terms used in the financing decision.
