At a glance

What Changed?

A member of the House of Councillors introduced a bill on 10 July 2026 proposing that government-bond interest and gains become eligible for tax-exempt treatment through NISA accounts, together with examination of an inheritance-tax exemption. The 221st Diet did not conclude the bill; it has not become law. [1] [2] [3]

Who Is Affected?

No immediate change to NISA investors, securities brokers, government-bond holders, tax administrators or heirs. Similar future legislation could affect their tax treatment and product processes. [1] [2] [3]

What Should Businesses Do Now?

Continue using existing NISA eligible-asset and tax rules. Do not assume that Japanese government bonds purchased in a NISA account qualify for the proposed new income-tax or inheritance-tax exemptions. [1] [2] [3]

Background & Purpose

The sponsors sought to expand the savings choices available through the Japanese NISA small-investment tax exemption system by adding Japanese government bonds as comparatively low-risk securities. Their official bill outline frames this as support for stable household asset formation and as a way to broaden domestic investor demand for government bonds, potentially contributing to smoother sovereign debt issuance. Those are legislative policy objectives, not findings that the existing NISA regime is unlawful.

The bill proposed that income tax relief under NISA should cover both interest income and transfer gains from Japanese government bonds, and that the government promptly prepare necessary implementing tax legislation while avoiding adverse effects on local government finances. It did not itself specify detailed annual account limits, purchase mechanics, broker procedures or an immediate right to an exemption.

A separate proposal asked the government to examine an inheritance-tax exemption for government bonds held in NISA accounts after the income-tax measures, and take any necessary action following that examination. The bill also called for review of measures to promote domestic investment through NISA. Both the inheritance-tax proposal and the domestic-investment discussion were forward-looking policy instructions, not existing tax relief.

Legislative & Regulatory History

Councillor Shuichi Harada submitted Councillors Bill No. 17 to the 221st Diet on 10 July 2026. The House of Representatives received the bill for preliminary review on 14 July 2026, but its progress record does not show final adoption, promulgation or an assigned law number.

The House of Representatives' official record lists the 221st Diet bill as '未了' (not concluded). The 221st session ended on 17 July 2026. The related House of Councillors bill page and the Legislative Bureau's official outline confirm the bill's subject, sponsor and the proposed government duties.

Under the outline, the bill itself would have commenced on the date of promulgation if it had been enacted, but the substantive NISA changes would have required prompt later legislative and administrative measures. Since it did not conclude, neither the proposed bond-income exemption nor the proposed inheritance-tax work programme arose as an enforceable duty under this bill.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Financial institutions and securities firms should keep current NISA product lists, eligibility controls, client tax disclosures and reporting rules unchanged. The proposal alone did not allow account holders to shelter government-bond coupon income or gains from tax. Investors should not treat general Japanese government bonds as NISA-eligible based solely on the July 2026 bill.

If a future proposal is enacted, financial intermediaries may need to review NISA onboarding, bond settlement, interest taxation and estate-documentation systems, and authorities would need to evaluate revenue implications. No concrete compliance cost or operative duty can be derived from a bill that the 221st Diet did not conclude.

Points to check

  • Confirm current NISA eligible assets and government-bond income treatment from enacted tax law and current broker documentation; do not cite Councillors Bill 17 as authority for an exemption.
  • Keep ordinary bond interest and gain reporting and withholding processes intact unless a later enacted amendment expressly changes them.
  • Monitor any newly introduced legislation and distinguish income-tax changes from the separate, unadopted proposal to study inheritance-tax relief.
