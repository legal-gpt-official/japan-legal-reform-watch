Background & Purpose

Waste lithium rechargeable batteries, and discarded products containing them, present a fire and propagation risk when crushed, mishandled or mixed into collection vehicles and treatment facilities. The Environment Ministry explicitly identifies recurring fires in those settings as the policy driver. General waste-handling duties already exist, but the proposed instruments would identify these batteries and battery-bearing products in more specific handling, separation and documentation requirements.

The proposed Cabinet Order would introduce parallel collection, transport, transfer and storage standards for these materials when they constitute municipal waste or industrial waste. Collection and transport would require measures to prevent mixing with other items and fire spread; rough handling such as dropping, impact and dragging would be prohibited under the draft. Transfer and storage would involve fire-prevention partitions or equivalent measures, and storage would avoid flames, sparks, hot objects and overheating.

The accompanying ministerial ordinance draft would embed the battery categories in storage-site signs, mandatory operator records, industrial-waste entrustment contracts, paper manifests and electronic-manifest registrations, export/import forms and relevant permits. It would also extend handling standards for hazardous used equipment containing lithium batteries and simplify the displayed waste-transport vessel permit number to its last six digits. These are draft requirements conditional on final adoption, not a claim that every waste generator already has each proposed duty.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The existing Waste Management and Public Cleansing Act sets the framework, with detailed standards in the 1971 Enforcement Order and Enforcement Regulations. The ministry's draft explains that particular waste streams such as asbestos- or mercury-containing waste are already singled out in implementing rules; it proposes a similarly explicit category for lithium-battery waste, reflecting different fire risks rather than altering the entire legal classification of waste.

On 29 October 2026 the ministry opened public consultation 195260074 on the Cabinet Order draft. Its official summary proposes new battery-waste collection, transport, transfer and storage standards for both municipal and industrial waste; comments are accepted until 29 November 2026 at 13:00 JST.

On 4 November 2026 the ministry opened related consultation 195260077 on ministerial ordinance amendments. Its four-page summary details signage, records, contract and manifest entries, permitting fields and transitional form rules; submissions close on 4 December 2026 at 00:00 JST. Both drafts tentatively envisage promulgation in late December 2026 and general implementation on 1 April 2027, with some purely editorial ordinance changes taking effect on promulgation.

Expected procedural steps

The usual procedure for this kind of instrument; not a forecast for this reform.

  • The ministry currently targets promulgation in late December 2026 and general commencement on 1 April 2027. These are projected dates in the drafts, not enacted commencement provisions; the ordinance's limited wording changes could commence on promulgation if adopted.
  • After consultation, the final Cabinet Order and ministerial ordinance, published response to comments, and any transitional instructions should be checked before procedures or contracts are changed as legally required.

Outstanding uncertainties

  • The final text, scope of battery products, operative thresholds if any, permitted alternative fire-prevention methods and exact commencement dates may change after consultation.
  • Implementation of the ministerial ordinance's detailed documentation requirements depends on the related Cabinet Order amendment; no final promulgated instrument has been established in the cited consultations.
  • The draft says existing submitted applications and issued permit forms would be deemed compliant with updated forms so that replacement is unnecessary; final transition language must be verified rather than treated as effective now.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Waste operators would need to assess segregation and ignition prevention during transport, transfer and storage. For industrial waste, the proposed ordinance would identify battery waste or battery-containing products in contract documents, manifests and, where relevant, electronic-manifest data. The requirement to state quantities in paper manifests applies to relevant industrial waste entries; it should not be generalized to all businesses regardless of waste stream or method.

Industrial-waste generators, recycling and logistics contractors, waste exporters/importers and permit holders should map which legal role and waste category applies to their activities. Potential costs include identifying affected waste, adjusting signage and segregation, training handlers and updating records, but the draft provides no firm aggregate compliance-cost estimate. Existing documents may benefit from the proposed no-replacement transitional rules.

Points to check

  • Identify discarded lithium batteries and products containing them in municipal, industrial or hazardous-used-equipment streams; document the applicable category and operator responsibilities.
  • Review separation, impact avoidance, heat controls, storage barriers and firefighting arrangements against the proposed Order while maintaining current safety controls.
  • Review industrial-waste contracts, paper or electronic manifests, permits and export/import forms for the proposed battery-related fields; distinguish the draft from current mandatory entries.
  • If affected, consider evidence-based comments by 29 November 2026 (Cabinet Order) or by 4 December 2026 at 00:00 JST (ordinance); check final texts, commencement and transitional provisions before implementation.
