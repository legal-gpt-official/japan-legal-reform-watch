At a glance

What Changed?

Cabinet Order No. 300 of 2026, promulgated on 28 September and effective 1 October 2026, raised the individual agricultural modernisation loan ceiling from JPY 18 million to JPY 200 million, aligned major repayment limits at 20 years and revised eligible organisation categories. Who Is Affected?

Eligible farm operators, agricultural corporations and qualifying agricultural-promotion organisations, agricultural cooperatives, Norinchukin-linked businesses and financial institutions providing interest-subsidised modernisation loans. [1] [3] [4] [5] [6]

What Should Businesses Do Now?

Use the amended eligibility and loan ceilings for new financing assessments, verify each borrower's business and ownership structure, and check lender approval, interest subsidies and programme conditions; the statutory ceiling is not a right to borrow that amount. [1] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Background & Purpose

Japan's Agricultural Modernisation Financing Act uses interest subsidies and eligible financing institutions to make longer-term, comparatively low-cost investment funding available for agricultural business development. It finances qualifying farm facilities and other modernisation activities, rather than offering unconditional grants. Before the 2026 change, the Enforcement Order fixed a ceiling of JPY 18 million for individual borrowers and many loan categories carried a 15-year repayment limit. These caps could become restrictive for more capital-intensive or larger-scale operations.

The Cabinet Legislation Bureau explained the parent amendment to Act No. 17 of 2026 as a response to growing financing demand arising from farm expansion, productivity improvements and higher-value agricultural businesses. Lawmakers therefore increased maximum aggregate lending amounts and broadened the group of organisations that can qualify, including certain bodies with Norinchukin Bank as a principal investor. The accompanying Cabinet Order translates these statutory policy choices into detailed borrower definitions, actual individual lending limits and maturities.

The 2026 Cabinet Order changes Enforcement Order Article 1's categories of qualifying corporations, including the voting-rights test for an agricultural-promotion stock company and a separate test for a qualifying membership company. It amends Article 2's repayment periods and Article 4's personal lending limit. It also makes a consequential adjustment to a 2011 order on special Great East Japan Earthquake agricultural financial measures. These are targeted changes to a financing programme, not an across-the-board legal entitlement to subsidised credit.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The prior statutory framework was designed around concessional long-term finance but did not fully match the scale of newer agricultural investments. In March 2026 the government submitted the parent Agricultural Modernisation Financing Act amendment to the 221st Diet; the Cabinet Legislation Bureau describes expanded financing and wider borrower eligibility as its express objectives. The amendment was enacted as Act No. 17 of 2026, and the government fixed its commencement at 1 October 2026.

MAFF consulted on the accompanying draft Cabinet Order from 17 August to 6 September 2026, with a shortened consultation period so the subordinate rules could operate when the parent Act entered into force. The consultation drew seven submissions. MAFF's results publication on 28 September reported no changes in response to those submissions, while the official results summary mentioned technical corrections to the published draft.

Cabinet Order No. 300 of 2026 was promulgated on 28 September 2026 and took effect on 1 October 2026. The final order changed the personal maximum from JPY 18 million to JPY 200 million and generally replaced former 15-year loan maturities with 20 years in the listed categories. Under revised Article 1, stock companies can include a qualifying majority held by agricultural persons or Norinchukin Bank, while the separate membership-company category uses a majority of executive members who are the listed agricultural persons. The same commencement date applies to its consequential earthquake-financing amendment.

Supplementary provision 2 of Cabinet Order No. 300 also updates Article 3(3) of the 2011 Great East Japan Earthquake agricultural-finance order. It deletes earlier wording that read a 15-year repayment term as 18 years and an 18-year term as 21 years in that special regime. Those textual substitutions referred to former base-period categories that the 2026 amendment has replaced. The consequential deletion keeps the exceptional order's references aligned with the revised financing schedule; it does not itself show that every earthquake-related borrower is entitled to a 21-year loan.

MAFF's public programme guidance, as checked on 12 November 2026, identifies an individual borrowing limit of JPY 200 million, JPY 700 million for agricultural corporations and groups, and a repayment period up to 20 years with a grace period up to seven years. Actual lending still depends on loan purpose, statutory borrower type, programme requirements and lender assessment.

Expected procedural steps

The usual procedure for this kind of instrument; not a forecast for this reform.

  • Borrowers and financing institutions can apply the revised statutory and Cabinet-Order framework when assessing qualifying new agricultural modernisation loans; specific approvals and contractual terms remain case-dependent.
  • Any later change to programme notices, applicable subsidised rates or administrative procedures requires a fresh check of MAFF guidance and the responsible financing institution. No further enactment step is required for this already effective order.

Outstanding uncertainties

  • The order sets eligibility definitions, maximum amounts and maturities; it does not establish that every borrower will be approved for the highest amount or receive the same interest subsidy.
  • An agricultural stock company's eligibility can depend on qualifying majority voting rights, while membership companies are tested under a different executive-member criterion. Ownership or legal form alone does not establish eligibility without satisfying all applicable conditions.
  • The applicable interest rate, security requirements, recognised investment purposes and availability of interest-subsidy measures for a particular transaction depend on the relevant programme guidance and lender assessment; they cannot be fixed from this order alone.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

For farmers planning machinery, facilities or expansion projects, moving the individual cap from JPY 18 million to JPY 200 million materially expands the maximum scale of potentially eligible modernisation finance. A repayment period of up to 20 years can spread principal repayments over a longer useful-life horizon. However, an individual farm's cash-flow profile, eligible spending, collateral and the financial institution's credit decision remain separate from these legal maxima.

For corporate groups and agricultural-promotion ventures, the amended borrower definitions matter as much as the amount. The Cabinet Order's stock-company test can take into account Norinchukin Bank's majority voting rights alongside the specified agricultural persons; the membership-company test focuses on who forms the majority of executive members. A structure that qualifies under one form should not automatically be assumed to qualify under the other. MAFF's programme page distinguishes JPY 700 million for agricultural corporations/groups from the JPY 200 million individual ceiling.

For lenders, agricultural cooperatives and compliance teams, the reform requires review of eligibility checklists, borrower ownership evidence, loan-purpose classification and maturity documents. MAFF's scheme operates through recognised financial channels and interest subsidies; neither the Act nor this order independently guarantees the stated ceiling or fixes a uniform transaction-specific price of credit.

Points to check

  • Confirm that the applicant qualifies as a farm operator, agricultural cooperative, specified stock company or membership company under amended Act Article 2 and Enforcement Order Article 1; document voting-rights or executive-member tests.
  • For an individual borrower, distinguish the post-1 October 2026 statutory ceiling of JPY 200 million from the old JPY 18 million figure and from the lender-approved loan amount.
  • For corporations and groups, use the separate JPY 700 million programme limit only where the applicant's category and conditions permit it; do not apply it to every legal entity automatically.
  • Classify the investment purpose under Enforcement Order Article 2, then check the applicable repayment and grace periods against the financing scheme; a 20-year maximum does not mean all loans are written for 20 years.
  • Confirm current subsidy conditions, indicative rates and any lending-institution approval processes directly with the relevant agricultural finance provider before making a financial commitment.
