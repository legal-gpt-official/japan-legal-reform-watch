Background & Purpose

Existing Japanese occupational-safety law requires hazard labeling on containers or packaging when specified hazardous chemicals are transferred or supplied, and requires notification of prescribed information through safety data sheets or equivalent means for designated substances. These existing duties must not be confused with the draft expansion of covered substances.

MHLW proposes to update the reference point for national GHS-based chemical classifications from 31 March 2025 to 31 March 2026, and to revise the annex of substances covered by labeling and SDS duties. The companion ordinance outline describes additions, removals and name changes in the substance list.

The draft Order outline proposes a 1 April 2029 commencement and a transitional rule for labeling of newly added substances already in existence on that date. The transitional rule concerns labeling for one year after the proposed commencement, not a general waiver of all SDS duties.

Legislative & Regulatory History

On 20 October 2026, MHLW opened consultation on a draft Cabinet Order and ministerial ordinance amending the chemical-substance scope of the Industrial Safety and Health rules.

The official Order and Ordinance outlines describe the proposed classification cutoff, changes to the listed substances, a planned February 2027 promulgation and a planned 1 April 2029 commencement. These dates appear in draft outlines and are not evidence of a promulgated instrument.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Chemical manufacturers, importers and distributors should identify which products may fall within the proposed updated lists and prepare to revise labels and safety data sheets if the changes are adopted. Existing duties continue to apply under current law.

Employers using affected chemicals should assess potential changes to hazard communication and inventory controls. The proposed labeling transition for existing stocks should not be read as suspending SDS notification requirements.

Points to check

  • Compare the official proposed substance annexes with current chemical inventories and supplier SDS records.
  • Separate duties already in force from duties that would arise only if the draft amendments are adopted.
  • Review the proposed labeling transition for newly covered existing stocks without assuming a blanket SDS exemption.
  • Consider comments before the deadline and monitor the final promulgation, effective dates and annex wording.
