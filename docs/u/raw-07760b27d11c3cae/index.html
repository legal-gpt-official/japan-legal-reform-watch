Background & Purpose

Cabinet Order No. 321 of 2026 amends the Enforcement Order under the Act on Rationalizing Energy Use and Transitioning to Non-Fossil Energy. It supports a framework concerning energy performance of gas and electric water-heating equipment; the detailed implementing standards are handled separately.

In its public-comment response, the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy clarified that the annual 3,000-unit threshold in Article 19 is part of the criteria for a recommendation under Article 150(1), not a threshold defining which products or businesses are covered by the overall scheme. The agency rejected the suggestion that the amendment narrows the scheme's scope.

An implementing ministerial ordinance and performance criteria for gas and electric water-heating equipment were separately opened for public comment on 22 October 2026. Consultation materials are drafts, not yet binding final requirements.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The domestic water-heater standards working group published its report on 22 May 2026. The Cabinet Order draft was open for comment from 15 June to 15 July 2026. The agency published the results on 29 September 2026, recording eight submissions and no revision of the draft in response.

The Cabinet Order was promulgated on 7 October 2026 as Cabinet Order No. 321 of 2026. The e-Gov amendment record states a scheduled commencement date of 1 April 2027.

On 22 October 2026, the agency opened a separate consultation on a ministerial ordinance amendment and water-heater performance criteria; comments are due by 20 November 2026.

  • The 3,000-unit recommendation threshold should not be confused with the coverage threshold of the scheme.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Manufacturers and importers of gas and electric water-heating equipment should distinguish product coverage and performance standards from the separate threshold governing possible ministerial recommendations. The public-comment response expressly says the latter does not reduce the scheme's regulatory scope.

Businesses should review affected product lines and the forthcoming final ordinance and notices before changing compliance specifications. The Cabinet Order's commencement does not by itself establish that every proposed performance benchmark has been finalized.

Points to check

  • Identify gas and electric water-heating product lines and map the existing legal duties applicable to each.
  • Separate Article 19 recommendation criteria from the scope of the efficiency framework; do not treat 3,000 units as a blanket exemption.
  • Review the implementing consultation and submit comments by 20 November 2026 if relevant.
  • Verify the final ordinance and notices, then prepare controls for the Cabinet Order's 1 April 2027 commencement.
