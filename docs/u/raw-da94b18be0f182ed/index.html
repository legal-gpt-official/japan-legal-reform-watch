Background & Purpose

Japan's regulated interconnection framework addresses the relationship between a carrier controlling designated fixed-network facilities and other carriers seeking access. Under Article 33 of the Telecommunications Business Act, interconnection terms are subject to a statutory framework rather than left solely to bilateral bargaining. The proposed amendment updates numerical inputs in the rules used to calculate regulated interconnection charges; it does not itself announce a new general obligation for all telecommunications businesses.

The consultation's 17-page draft replaces figures for network lengths, asset valuation, facility-maintenance costs, land-price adjustment factors, network-equipment ratios and economic useful lives. For example, the feeder conduit total changes from 118,639 km to 116,822 km, the QoS control coefficient from 1.27 to 1.26, and the IP data packet factor from 108 to 116 packets per second per Mbps. These are proposed model inputs, not direct retail price changes.

The regulatory logic is to maintain cost-based access calculations using specified assumptions. The draft tables demonstrate changes to those assumptions, but the consultation materials reviewed here do not establish the empirical cause of each adjustment or quantify its aggregate effect on individual carriers' interconnection charges. Any claim of a net increase or decrease requires calculation using the complete model.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The underlying Interconnection Charges Rules were adopted as Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications Ordinance No. 64 of 2000 (Heisei 12) under the Telecommunications Business Act. The current proposal relies expressly on Article 33(4)(ii), which concerns the regulatory basis for calculating interconnection charges.

On 3 November 2026, MIC opened public consultation on a further amendment to the rules, with comments due by 2 December 2026. The proposal consists predominantly of detailed numerical revisions in attached tables, rather than a wholesale replacement of the interconnection regime.

The draft supplementary provisions envisage a general commencement on 1 April 2027, while provisions allowing advance procedural steps would commence on promulgation. Before commencement, the designated operator could seek approval to amend existing interconnection terms; where a pre-commencement application remains undecided after commencement, the draft contains a temporary deeming rule pending the decision.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

The designated fixed-network operator would need to test its cost-model calculations and approved interconnection terms against the proposed numerical inputs. Changes to facility lengths, maintenance ratios and economic useful lives can alter calculated cost allocation, but direction and magnitude cannot be inferred from selected figures alone.

Interconnecting operators should evaluate the impact on their regulated wholesale charges and contract economics without assuming the draft has immediate legal effect. The supplementary provisions are relevant to the timing of approval applications and temporary treatment of existing terms.

Points to check

  • Recalculate the complete interconnection model using both current and proposed numerical tables; retain a traceable comparison.
  • Identify any existing approved interconnection terms requiring an amendment application if the final rules follow the draft.
  • Consider submitting evidence-based comments by 2 December 2026 on cost assumptions or transitional arrangements.
  • Check the promulgated text and the applicable approval decision before treating new rates or inputs as effective.
