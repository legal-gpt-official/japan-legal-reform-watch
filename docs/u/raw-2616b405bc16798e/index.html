What Changed?

A draft amendment to the Telecommunications Numbering Plan would tighten identity verification for fixed-number call-forwarding customers, aligning with anti-money-laundering reforms. Consultation has closed; adoption is unconfirmed. [1] [2] [3]

Who Is Affected?

Telecommunications operators providing call forwarding that uses fixed telephone numbers, and customers using those services, would be affected. [1] [2] [3]

Background & Purpose

Telephone call forwarding can allow a user to present a fixed geographic number without operating at the apparent location. Identity checks are therefore significant to the integrity of number use and to controls against impersonation. The ministry explicitly links the draft to stronger customer verification under the amended anti-money-laundering rules, rather than to a general redesign of telephone numbers.

The related anti-money-laundering implementing order is scheduled to change non-face-to-face verification from 1 April 2027. The ministry explains that image uploads and copies of identity documents can be vulnerable to forgery or impersonation. The draft would align the numbering plan's checks for fixed-number forwarding, while preserving specified alternative routes for people without chip-equipped identity documents.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The existing Telecommunications Numbering Plan specifies methods for verifying the identity of end users of particular forwarding services. A separate amendment to anti-money-laundering implementing rules prompted the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications to propose corresponding adjustments for telecom providers.

The ministry announced the proposed amendment on 24 September 2026 and accepted comments from 25 September to 26 October 2026. The consultation has ended, but the official materials reviewed do not establish a final amended notice or its effective date.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Fixed-number call-forwarding operators may need to revise remote onboarding, chip-reading workflows, accepted identity documents and the evidential record of verification. The proposed approach is more restrictive than reliance on an uploaded copy alone, but final obligations depend on the published notice.

Business customers using forwarded fixed numbers may face changed identification procedures at service sign-up or review. The proposal is not a blanket rule for every mobile or broadband customer: the official explanation specifies forwarding services using fixed numbers.

Points to check

  • Identify customer-verification methods used for call-forwarding services that employ fixed telephone numbers.
  • Compare chip-based and non-chip verification routes with the draft and document operational dependencies before final adoption.
