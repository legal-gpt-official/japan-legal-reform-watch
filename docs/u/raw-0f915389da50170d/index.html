Background & Purpose

Japan's defined-contribution pension system relies on recordkeeping and benefit-claim processes that historically specified documents or paper notices. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare identifies the administrative burden on recordkeeping administrators and related institutions as the immediate reason for this draft, rather than a change in pension eligibility or contribution limits.

The proposal would permit electronic submission of record-management requests, transfers of individually managed assets, old-age benefit claims and specified withdrawal claims. It would also remove the express paper requirement for certain benefit notifications and waive separate proof-of-birth-date attachments where a signature electronic certificate has verified the date. These are proposed procedural options, not already-effective entitlements.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The ministry has separately maintained the defined-contribution pension system's operational interpretations and Q&A, including materials current as of October 2026. The latest proposal targets transaction-level friction in the existing administration, so its rationale should not be confused with the broader 2025 pension reform.

On 26 October 2026, the ministry opened consultation on amendments to the 2001 enforcement regulation. Its official outline proposes promulgation in late December 2026 and commencement on 1 January 2027; both remain plans pending final adoption and publication.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Recordkeeping administrators may need to revise participant portals, electronic-signature validation, notice delivery and audit trails. The draft would allow electronic methods for specified transactions, but does not establish that every administrator must offer every channel immediately.

Employers sponsoring corporate plans should distinguish the administrator's operational changes from the employer's existing plan-document and communication duties. Participants may gain less paper-intensive procedures only after the relevant systems and final rules are in place.

Points to check

  • Map each proposed electronic claim, transfer and notification to the current paper workflow and responsible recordkeeper.
  • Check whether signature electronic certificates can verify birth dates without an extra document, and assess retention and security controls.
