Summary

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare published a draft amending Notice for public comment on 2026-10-20, with a stated deadline of 2026-11-18 16:00 JST. The proposal would amend the substances designated by the Minister under Article 594-2, paragraph 1 of the Ordinance on Industrial Safety and Health as clearly posing risks of skin or eye damage, or health impairment through skin absorption or penetration.

Business Impact

Employers handling substances covered by these designations may be affected by changes to the designated substances; the supplied information does not identify the proposed additions or removals.

Background & Purpose

Japan's occupational chemical controls increasingly rely on risk assessment and hazard communication rather than only the substances covered by older, substance-specific regulations. Under the current Industrial Safety and Health Regulation, employers handling designated substances that can harm skin or eyes or enter through the skin must, subject to stated exceptions, ensure appropriate impermeable protective equipment is used.

The ministry explains that national GHS classifications are updated as hazard evidence changes. Its proposal would remove four substances, including lead(II) chloride, whose classifications no longer meet the relevant category 1 criteria, and would refresh the scope using later national classification results. The change reflects evidence-based updating, not a declaration that all listed chemicals have become safe.

Legislative & Regulatory History

The MHLW's broader chemical-management reform strengthened workplace risk assessment, safety-data-sheet communication and controls for dermal exposure. The 2025 ministerial notice designated substances using national GHS classification results; a static list can become inaccurate as those classifications are revised.

On 20 October 2026 the ministry opened consultation on a staged revision. Its outline proposes one set of exclusions from the date of the eventual notice, and a later update based on classifications through March 2026 to apply on 1 April 2028. The ministry links the later date to allowing time for safety-data-sheet preparation and other business adjustments.

Business Impact & Practical Considerations

Manufacturers, laboratories and maintenance operators should use the current legally designated list for present protective-equipment duties, while mapping the proposed changes to their inventories and safety-data sheets. A removal from this particular designation does not necessarily eliminate obligations under other occupational chemical rules.

The proposed two-stage approach may change which substances trigger the specific impermeable protective-equipment duty at different times. Businesses should avoid assuming that a new GHS classification instantly amends the legally designated list before the relevant notice applies.

Points to check

  • Cross-check the current workplace chemical inventory against the existing skin/eye hazard designation and the draft exclusions.
  • Identify any substances likely to enter the later classification-based scope, and schedule SDS and protective-equipment reviews without treating proposed dates as enacted.
