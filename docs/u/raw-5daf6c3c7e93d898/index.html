Background & Purpose

The bill's sponsors argued that living costs and nominal wage growth had exposed weaknesses in individual inhabitant-tax deductions and incentives to work longer hours. These were policy assertions advanced to support a proposed framework, not findings that existing assessments were unlawful. The House subsequently withdrew the bill in October 2026, so none of its proposed measures became binding law.

As originally submitted, the bill called for an increase in the maximum basic deduction so that it and the minimum employment-income deduction would total 1,780,000 yen for the inhabitant-tax income levy from fiscal 2027. The proposed benchmark referred to average regional minimum-wage changes since fiscal 1995. The measure was never enacted; this is neither a present inhabitant-tax threshold nor a general income-tax or social-insurance exemption.

It also contemplated adding dependants younger than 16 to the inhabitant-tax dependent deduction from fiscal 2028, examining exemptions for specified vulnerable groups, and central-government measures to compensate municipalities for lost local revenue. These were conditional policy proposals in a withdrawn bill; they create no present entitlement, tax liability change or municipal reimbursement duty.

Legislative & Regulatory History

Four House members introduced House Bill No. 31 in the 221st Diet on 15 July 2026, and it was referred to the Committee on Internal Affairs and Communications on 23 July 2026. An older bill-progress page still shows consideration during recess, but that is superseded by the 222nd Diet's subsequent withdrawal record.

At its 22 October 2026 meeting in the 222nd Diet, the House committee unanimously consented to the sponsors' request to withdraw House Bill No. 31. The House's 222nd Diet bill list records its status as withdrawn. No enactment or promulgation occurred, and the proposed fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2028 tax changes were not implemented through this bill.

The withdrawn proposal had contemplated a framework directing the government to pursue subsequent amendments, including reconsideration of personal deductions by the end of 2028. Withdrawal terminated that proposal's legislative path; any revival would require another legislative initiative and enactment of appropriate tax-law amendments. No such revival is established by the cited sources.
